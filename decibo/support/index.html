Help with Decibo for iPhone

How accurate is it?

A phone microphone is not a certified sound level meter, so Decibo's readings are estimates. Decibo uses the iPhone's measurement mode, which turns off automatic gain and noise processing. Very quiet rooms (under about 35 dB) and very loud places are the least exact, and a case or cover over the microphone changes the reading. Decibo is not for legal or workplace compliance measurements.

Calibrate against a real meter

With Decibo Pro, open Settings, Calibration. Play a steady sound, measure it with a calibrated meter and with Decibo held in the same spot, and move the offset until they match.

dBA or dBZ? Fast or Slow?

dBA follows how loud sound seems to people and is what hearing guidance uses. dBZ counts every frequency equally, including deep rumble. Fast follows the sound closely; Slow gives a steadier number. Tap the chips at the top of the meter to switch.

What do the hearing messages mean?

They use the NIOSH recommended limit: 85 dBA for 8 hours a day, with the safe time halving for every 3 dB louder. At 91 dBA that is about 2 hours. A saved session's noise dose shows how much of a day's allowance it used.

The meter says the microphone is off

Open iPhone Settings, Apps, Decibo, and turn on Microphone. The meter's Open Settings button takes you there.

It paused during a call

Calls and some apps take the microphone. Decibo pauses, keeps your min, average and max, and carries on when it can. Tap Resume if it doesn't.

Measure a whole night

With Decibo Pro, tap Timed session on the meter, pick a length up to 8 hours and a name such as "Bedroom at night". You can lock your iPhone; measuring carries on and the session saves itself at the end. Keep it on the charger.

Add the widget

Touch and hold your Home Screen, tap Edit, then Add Widget, and choose Decibo. It shows your last session; tap it to open the meter.

Restore Decibo Pro

Open Settings in Decibo and tap Restore purchase, signed in with the Apple Account you bought it with.
