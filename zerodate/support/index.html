Help with Zerodate for iPhone

How is my date worked out?

Zerodate takes each debt's balance, interest rate and minimum payment, adds your monthly extra, and simulates every month: interest is added, minimums are paid, and the extra goes to one debt at a time. When a debt is paid off, its payment rolls over to the next. The month the last balance reaches zero is your debt-free date. Real lenders may calculate interest slightly differently, so treat it as a close estimate.

Snowball or avalanche?

Snowball pays the smallest balance first, for quick wins. Avalanche pays the highest interest rate first, which usually costs the least. "Compare strategies" shows both side by side for your own debts.

Log a payment

Tap "Log a payment", choose the debt and the amount. Use Lump sum for extra one-off payments, or New balance to match your statement. Every entry can be edited or deleted from the debt's history.

"Not yet" instead of a date

This means the payments don't cover the interest, so the balance would never reach zero. Raise the minimum on that debt or add a monthly extra.

Reminders don't arrive

In Zerodate, open Settings, Reminders, and turn them on. If iPhone notifications are off for Zerodate, open iPhone Settings, Apps, Zerodate, Notifications, and turn on Allow Notifications.

Add the widget

Touch and hold your Home Screen or Lock Screen, tap Edit (or Customise), then Add Widget, and choose Zerodate. It shows your debt-free date, the days to go and how much is paid off.

Zerodate Pro

Free plans up to 3 unpaid debts at a time. Zerodate Pro is one payment: unlimited debts, your own payoff order, the what-if slider and CSV export. To restore it, open Settings in Zerodate and tap Restore purchase, signed in with the Apple Account you bought it with.

Not financial advice

Zerodate is a calculator. For advice about your situation, talk to a licensed financial adviser or a free financial counsellor.
